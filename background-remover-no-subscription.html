Background removal without a monthly bill

If you've searched for a background remover recently, you'll have noticed something: almost all of them want either a subscription or a credit balance. This is an honest look at why, what the free ones actually cost you, and what to look for if you'd rather just buy a tool and own it.

Disclosure up front: we make one of the options discussed here. Read it accordingly — the comparison is still useful, and we've tried to be fair about where our own product isn't the answer.

Why everything is a subscription

There's a real reason, not just greed. If a tool processes your photo on a server, that server costs money every single time — GPU time, bandwidth in, bandwidth out, storage while it works. A cloud background remover has a genuine per-photo cost, so it has to charge per photo, whether it dresses that up as credits, a monthly allowance, or a "free" tier that watermarks the output.

The interesting part is that this cost is optional. Apple's Vision framework has done subject segmentation on-device since iOS 15, and modern Android has equivalents. The chip in a phone from 2021 can do this work in a fraction of a second, for free, forever. A tool that uses your device's own processor has no marginal cost, and therefore has no reason to meter you.

So when you pay monthly for background removal, you're mostly paying for the architectural decision to do it somewhere else.

The four models, and what each really costs

1. Free web tools

Upload a photo, get a cutout. The catch is usually one of: a watermark, a hard resolution cap (often 500 px, which is useless for a listing), a daily limit, or heavy advertising.

The less obvious cost is your photos. You are uploading images of your stock to a company whose business model you haven't read. For most items that's harmless; if you sell anything with a serial number, a document, a valuation, or a customer's property in shot, it deserves a moment's thought.

Fine for: one photo, occasionally, at low resolution.

2. Credit systems

You buy a pack of credits, each photo costs one. It sounds fair until you have a listing day: forty items, three angles each, and you're re-buying credits at 2pm with a table full of stock waiting.

Credits also encourage the wrong behaviour — you start rationing, shooting fewer angles, and skipping the reshoot you know a photo needs.

Fine for: genuinely occasional, unpredictable use.

3. Monthly subscriptions

Typically somewhere between the price of a coffee and the price of a lunch each month, often with a photo allowance on top. Over three years, a modest subscription quietly becomes several hundred pounds for a feature your phone already has.

The other thing to weigh: subscriptions can raise prices, change their allowances, or shut down entirely, and your workflow goes with them.

Fine for: businesses where it's a rounding error and the tool does far more than cutouts.

4. Buy it once

A one-time purchase, usually an app that runs on your own device. No account, no metering, no renewal. The trade-off is that it's tied to a platform — an on-device tool can only exist where the on-device machine learning exists.

Fine for: anyone who processes photos regularly and stays on one platform.

What to check before you commit

QuestionWhy it matters
Is there a hard photo cap?Caps always bite on the busiest day, never the quiet one.
What resolution is the output?You want a 2000 px long edge. Many free tiers cap far lower and it's not obvious until you download.
Are my photos uploaded?Determines whether it works offline, how fast it is, and who else has your images.
Can it batch?One-at-a-time is fine for five items and unbearable for fifty.
Does it do backdrop and shadow, or just cut out?A bare cutout on transparent still needs another tool before it's listing-ready.
What aspect ratios?If it only outputs the original shape, you're cropping everything by hand afterwards.
Does it need an account?An account means a password, a marketing list, and a thing that can be lost.
What happens if they shut down?With a cloud tool, everything stops. With a purchased app, it keeps working.

Where StudioDrop sits

StudioDrop is the fourth model: buy it once, runs on your device, no account and no metering. £7.99 for iPhone and iPad, £19.99 for Mac.

It does the whole job rather than just the cutout:

  • Removes the background using Apple's on-device machine learning
  • Drops the item onto white, transparent, nine curated colours, or any colour you like
  • Adds a soft studio shadow so it doesn't look pasted
  • Auto-enhances sharpness, brightness and colour
  • Crops to square, portrait, tall, vertical or wide — always 2000 px on the long edge
  • Batches a whole shoot at once (500+ at a time on Mac)
  • Never uploads anything, works with no signal, no account, no watermark

Where it isn't the answer

Being straight about this, because a wrong purchase helps nobody:

  • You're not on Apple hardware. There's no Windows or Android version and no plan for one — the entire approach depends on Apple's on-device engine.
  • You need one cutout a month. A free web tool will do, and you shouldn't pay for this.
  • You need heavy manual retouching. StudioDrop is automatic by design. For hand-refining tricky edges you want a full editor.
  • You mostly shoot hair, fur, mesh or clear glass. These are hard for every automatic tool including this one; see the photo guide for what helps.
  • Your Mac is Intel. The Mac app needs Apple Silicon.

The maths, if it helps

A £6-a-month subscription is £72 a year, or £216 over three years. StudioDrop Go is £7.99 once. If you list regularly on an iPhone, the purchase pays for itself inside the second month and then costs nothing for as long as you own the phone.

If you list rarely, that maths doesn't work in our favour, and you should use something free.
